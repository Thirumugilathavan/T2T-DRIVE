# T2T DRIVE — Field Research & Ethnographic Interview Plan
## Qualitative & Quantitative Study of In-Cabin Friction: Salem & Chennai Auto Hubs

### 1. Research Objectives
1. Understand the exact mechanics and pain points of driver-passenger fare negotiation and mid-trip route alteration in Tamil Nadu tier-1 (Chennai) and tier-2 (Salem) urban transit.
2. Evaluate willingness of auto drivers to adopt voice AI and digital trip confirmation.
3. Assess passenger comfort with scanning in-cabin QR codes without app installation.
4. Establish baseline metrics for fare disputes, unpaid mid-trip additions, and reconciliation disputes with vehicle owners.

---

### 2. Participant Sampling & Field Locations

#### Target Cohort:
- **10 Auto-Rickshaw Drivers:**
  - 5 in Chennai (Central Station, T Nagar Bus Terminus, Guindy Metro, Airport Stand).
  - 5 in Salem (New Bus Stand, Old Bus Stand, Junction Railway Station, Suramangalam).
  - Mix of owner-drivers (4) and rental/contract drivers paying daily bhatta (6).
  - Age distribution: 24–58 years. Experience: 2 to 25+ years.
- **5 Regular Commuters / Passengers:**
  - 3 in Chennai (Tech commuter, college student, senior citizen).
  - 2 in Salem (Local market trader, inter-district bus commuter).

---

### 3. Key Research Questions

1. **The Bargaining Dance:**
   - *How is initial fare negotiated? How often do passengers propose a lower fare or ask for metre + extra?*
2. **Mid-Trip Scope Creep:**
   - *How often do passengers say "Vazhila konjam ATM-la niruthunga" or change destination midway? How often does this cause arguments at the end of the trip?*
3. **Payment Collection & Leakage:**
   - *What percentage of fares are paid via UPI (GPay/PhonePe) vs. Cash? How often do passengers walk away claiming "UPI done" when the notification hasn't arrived?*
4. **Owner-Driver Reconciliation:**
   - *How do rental drivers calculate and prove their net daily earnings to the auto owner after fuel and bhatta?*
5. **Technology Readiness:**
   - *Can drivers use smartphone touchscreens while steering an auto-rickshaw? Would a Tamil voice co-pilot make their work safer and easier?*

---

### 4. Semi-Structured Interview Guide (Driver Protocol)

#### Section A: Profile & Daily Work (10 Mins)
- How many trips do you complete on an average day? (Typical response: 12–18 trips).
- Do you own the auto or pay daily bhatta? How much is the daily rental? (₹250–₹400/day).
- What is your daily fuel expenditure? (LPG/CNG: ₹350–₹500; Petrol: ₹600–₹800).

#### Section B: Passenger Bargaining & Route Alteration (15 Mins)
- When a passenger asks you to add a stop (e.g. buying medicines, picking up food), how do you decide how much extra to charge?
- How often does a passenger argue at drop-off that the extra stop was "on the way" and refuse to pay extra?
- Have you ever faced disputes where the passenger agreed to ₹200 initially but paid only ₹150 at the end?

#### Section C: T2T DRIVE Demonstration & Usability Feedback (15 Mins)
- *Demonstrate Phase 4/6 Voice Co-pilot:* Say in Tanglish: `"Central Station-ku trip start pannu, fare 250"`. Show the voice audio confirmation and automatic QR code generation.
- *Demonstrate Mid-Trip Voice Change:* Say: `"Destination maathunga, Airport terminal 2"`. Show how both driver and passenger receive the update.
- *Driver Questions:*
  1. Did you understand the Tamil/Tanglish voice prompt?
  2. Would having an agreed digital record prevent passenger arguments?
  3. Would you feel safe answering "Aama" (Yes) with your voice while driving?

---

### 5. Semi-Structured Interview Guide (Passenger Protocol)

1. When you board an auto-rickshaw, what makes you distrust the quoted fare?
2. If the driver has a laminated QR code on the partition saying *"Scan for Digital Receipt & Agreed Fare"*, would you scan it with your phone camera?
3. If scanning opened a simple webpage without downloading an app or logging in, would you find that convenient?
4. If you ask the auto driver to change destination, would you prefer seeing the updated agreed fare confirmed on your phone screen?

### 7. Expected Insights & Design Implication Deliverables
1. **Validation of Zero-App Passenger Entry:** Passengers strongly resist downloading dedicated apps for street-hailed auto rides. The mobile web QR link (`passenger.html?trip_id=...&token=...`) removes all friction.
2. **Audio Affirmation for Drivers:** Drivers cannot look down at screens at busy intersections. The one-word voice confirmation firewall is the single most important safety feature.
3. **Receipt as Dispute Shield:** Providing passengers a timestamped digital receipt showing pickup, drop, intermediate stops, and agreed fare eliminates the classic "He overcharged me" complaint at the curb.
